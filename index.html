Home base

Island Beach Lodge

A 14-bed beach house on the calm side of the Dudley Peninsula, facing north over Eastern Cove. It's close to the ferry and Penneshaw, and within 40 minutes of Kingscote. The wild west end is a full day out.

Things to do

Ordered from nearest to furthest. National parks sell a Kangaroo Island Tour Pass ($95 adult, $262 family, in person only) that covers Flinders Chase, the Seal Bay boardwalk, Kelly Hill Caves and the Cape Willoughby and Cape Borda lighthouses. It pays off if you do four or more.

Walking tracks

Walks you can do on your own, checked against Parks SA's 2026 maps and alerts. Grades run from 1 (a flat path anyone can manage) to 5 (experienced walkers only). February is hot, so walk early, carry water, and check parks.sa.gov.au alerts the morning you go.

Walking safely in February

  • Parks close on Catastrophic fire-danger days and may close on Extreme days. Check the CFS app or 1800 362 361.
  • There's no drinking water on the tracks. Carry 3 L each for anything over an hour, plus a hat and sunscreen.
  • Snakes are active in summer. Stay on the track, wear closed shoes, and pack a compression bandage.
  • Use the boot-cleaning stations; they stop a root-rot fungus spreading.
  • West of Parndana, only Telstra has any signal, and it's patchy. Tell someone your route before remote walks.

Not an option this trip

  • KI Wilderness Trail (5 days, 66 km) needs a booking ($220). You can walk the sections that share park tracks, listed above, with ordinary park entry.
  • Breakneck River Hike is no longer on the Parks SA map.
  • Investigator Trail (Pelican Lagoon) is badly overgrown.
  • Cape Torrens Wilderness Protection Area is closed until further notice.
  • Cape Gantheaume Coastal Trek is a 2-day unmarked route for experienced walkers only.

Birdwatching

February is a good month. Migratory shorebirds from Siberia and Alaska are feeding up before flying north, hooded plover chicks are on the beaches, and the endangered glossy black-cockatoo (about 450 birds, all on this island) is starting to nest. Waders feed best at low or rising tide.

Watch responsibly

  • Stay 30 m from feeding glossy black-cockatoos and 50 m from drinking or nesting ones. Never play call recordings.
  • Hooded plovers nest just above the high-tide mark. Walk below it, and respect fenced areas.
  • Bird flu (H5) has been found in terns and sea lions on the island. Don't touch sick or dead birds or animals, and report them on 1800 675 888.

Bring or download

  • Binoculars, plus a scope for the tidal flats.
  • Chris Baxter's Birds of Kangaroo Island photographic guide (2015).
  • The BirdLife KI Bird ID booklet (free PDF from the KI Landscape Board).
  • eBird hotspots: American River, Duck Lagoon, Cygnet River Mouth, Bay of Shoals, Chapman River Estuary.

Costs nothing but fuel

Free things to do

A lot of the best of Kangaroo Island is free: the beaches, the lookouts, most of the walks, the farm gates and the night sky. Minutes are the drive from the lodge.

Not free, despite what some older guides say: Hope Cottage museum ($10), Penneshaw Maritime Museum (≈ $4), and most cellar-door tastings ($7–15, often waived if you buy).

EV charging

The recommended route has fast chargers most of the way. The one real gap is about 200 km with no fast charging from Mount Gambier through Coonawarra and Robe to Kingston SE, so leave Mount Gambier full and charge overnight in Robe. On Kangaroo Island there's one fast charger, in Penneshaw, 17 minutes from the lodge. The lodge itself appears to have two chargers. All chargers are on both maps; switch on the EV layer.

On Kangaroo Island

Can you do Flinders Chase and back?

  • The full day out (lodge → Admirals Arch → Remarkable Rocks → lodge) is about 290 km on sealed roads, using roughly 46–58 kWh at highway speed with the air-con on.
  • Fine from 100% for cars with about 70 kWh or more usable. Tight for 50–60 kWh cars.
  • For a smaller battery, top up at Penneshaw the day before, and stop at the airport chargers on the way home.

Taking an EV on the ferry

  • Declare the EV when you book. EV spaces are limited, so book early.
  • Expect a battery and temperature check before boarding. No warning lights or open recalls.
  • No charging on board. Board with a healthy charge (60% or more is sensible).
  • EV rules are under review for the new ferries. Ask SeaLink (13 13 01) when booking.

Pack and pay

  • A Type 2 cable: every RAA slow charger needs your own.
  • A 10 A portable charger as backup: about 12–15 km of range per hour.
  • Apps: Chargefox (pays for RAA and NRMA), Evie, Tesla, PlugShare. RAA fast charging costs 68–76c/kWh, with idle fees.
  • The old council chargers in Kingscote, Parndana, American River and Penneshaw have been removed, even though some maps still show them.
